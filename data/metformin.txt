Metformin. Uses: Metformin is a medicine for type 2 diabetes. It lowers blood sugar by reducing the sugar made by the liver and helping the body respond better to insulin. It is often used together with diet and exercise.
How to take: Take metformin with or just after meals to reduce stomach side effects. The dose is usually increased slowly by the doctor. Do not change the dose without medical advice. Extended-release tablets must be swallowed whole.
Side effects: Common side effects are nausea, diarrhoea, stomach pain, loss of appetite, and a metallic taste, especially at the start of treatment. Long-term use may lower vitamin B12 levels. A rare but serious side effect is lactic acidosis. Seek urgent help for fast breathing, severe tiredness, muscle pain, stomach pain with vomiting, or feeling very unwell.
Warnings: Tell your doctor if you have kidney or liver problems, because metformin may not be suitable. Heavy alcohol use increases the risk of lactic acidosis. The medicine may need to be stopped temporarily before some scans using contrast dye, or before surgery, or during serious illness with dehydration.
Interactions: Alcohol, some contrast dyes, and certain medicines that affect the kidneys can increase the risk of side effects. Always tell your doctor and pharmacist that you take metformin before starting a new medicine.
