Diclofenac. Uses: Diclofenac is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID) used to relieve pain and inflammation in conditions such as arthritis, back pain, muscle and joint injuries, period pain, and dental pain. It is sold under brand names such as Cataflam and Voltaren.
How to take: Take it with or after food to protect the stomach, swallowing tablets whole with water. Use the lowest effective dose for the shortest time. The dose must follow the doctor's or product label instructions.
Who should not take it: Do not take diclofenac if you have a stomach ulcer or bleeding, serious heart disease, severe kidney or liver problems, or have had asthma or an allergic reaction after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs. Avoid it in the last 3 months of pregnancy.
Side effects: Common side effects are indigestion, stomach pain, nausea, diarrhoea, and headache. Stop the medicine and seek urgent help for black stools, vomiting blood, chest pain, or signs of allergic reaction. Long-term use may increase the risk of heart problems and kidney damage.
Interactions: Do not combine diclofenac with ibuprofen, aspirin, or other NSAIDs because the risk of stomach bleeding increases. Taking it with warfarin raises the risk of serious bleeding. It may reduce the effect of blood pressure medicines and can raise methotrexate levels.
