Atorvastatin. Uses: Atorvastatin is a statin medicine that lowers cholesterol and fats in the blood. It is used to reduce the risk of heart attack and stroke in people at risk, and is usually taken together with a healthy diet. It is sold under brand names such as Lipitor.
How to take: Take it once a day at the same time each day, with or without food, as prescribed by the doctor. Do not stop it without medical advice, even if you feel well.
Warnings: Tell your doctor if you have liver disease or are pregnant or breastfeeding, as statins are not suitable in these cases. Drinking large amounts of grapefruit juice can raise atorvastatin levels, so avoid it. Regular blood tests may be needed to check the liver.
Side effects: Common side effects include headache, stomach upset, constipation or diarrhoea, and mild muscle aches. Seek medical help quickly for unexplained severe muscle pain, weakness, or dark urine, as these can be signs of a rare serious muscle problem.
Interactions: Some antibiotics such as clarithromycin, certain antifungal medicines, and some other medicines can increase the risk of muscle side effects. Atorvastatin can slightly affect the level of warfarin or digoxin. Always tell your doctor and pharmacist about all medicines you take.
