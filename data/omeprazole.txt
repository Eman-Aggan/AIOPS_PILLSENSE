Omeprazole. Uses: Omeprazole is a proton pump inhibitor (PPI) that reduces the amount of acid made by the stomach. It treats heartburn, acid reflux, stomach ulcers, and protects the stomach in people who take NSAIDs for long periods.
How to take: Swallow the capsule or tablet whole with water, usually once a day before a meal, most often before breakfast. Do not crush or chew it. It may take a few days for full effect.
Warnings: See a doctor if you have trouble swallowing, unexplained weight loss, persistent vomiting, or black stools, because these can be signs of a more serious condition. Long-term use may lower magnesium and vitamin B12 levels and may slightly increase the risk of bone fractures, so long-term use should be reviewed by a doctor.
Side effects: Common side effects include headache, nausea, diarrhoea, constipation, stomach pain, and wind. Contact a doctor for severe diarrhoea, a skin rash, or signs of an allergic reaction.
Interactions: Omeprazole can reduce the effect of the antiplatelet medicine clopidogrel, so this combination is usually avoided. It can raise methotrexate levels and may affect the levels of digoxin and some other medicines. Tell your doctor or pharmacist about everything you take.
