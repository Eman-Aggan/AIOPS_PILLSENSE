Warfarin. Uses: Warfarin is an anticoagulant (blood thinner). It prevents harmful blood clots in people with atrial fibrillation, artificial heart valves, deep vein thrombosis, or pulmonary embolism.
How to take: Take warfarin at the same time every day, exactly as prescribed. The dose is adjusted using a blood test called INR, so regular blood tests are essential. Never change the dose or stop the medicine without medical advice.
Diet and lifestyle: Foods rich in vitamin K, such as spinach, kale, and broccoli, can reduce the effect of warfarin. Keep your intake of these foods consistent rather than suddenly increasing or decreasing it. Alcohol and cranberry juice can change the effect, so limit them and ask your doctor.
Warnings: The main risk is bleeding. Seek urgent medical help for severe bleeding, black or bloody stools, blood in urine, a nosebleed that does not stop, vomiting blood, or a serious head injury. Tell any doctor, dentist, or pharmacist that you take warfarin.
Interactions: Warfarin interacts with many medicines. Aspirin, ibuprofen and other NSAIDs increase bleeding risk and should not be taken without medical advice. Many antibiotics, including amoxicillin, can increase the effect of warfarin. Regular use of paracetamol can also raise INR. Herbal products and supplements can interfere too, so always ask a pharmacist before taking any new medicine.
