Aspirin. Uses: Aspirin is a pain reliever, fever reducer, and anti-inflammatory medicine. At low doses (usually 75 mg daily), a doctor may prescribe it to prevent blood clots, heart attacks, and strokes in people at risk.
How to take: For pain or fever, adults usually take 300 mg to 900 mg every 4 to 6 hours, with a maximum of 4000 mg (4 g) in 24 hours. Take it with food and a full glass of water. Low-dose aspirin for heart protection should only be taken if a doctor has recommended it.
Who should not take it: Children and teenagers under 16 should not take aspirin unless a doctor prescribes it, because of the risk of Reye's syndrome, a rare but serious condition. Do not take it if you have a stomach ulcer, a bleeding disorder, or an allergy to aspirin or NSAIDs. Avoid it in pregnancy unless advised by a doctor.
Side effects: Common side effects include indigestion and stomach irritation. Aspirin increases the risk of bleeding, including stomach bleeding. Seek urgent help if you notice black stools, vomiting blood, or unusual bruising.
Interactions: Aspirin taken with warfarin greatly increases the risk of serious bleeding. Taking aspirin with ibuprofen or other NSAIDs increases the risk of stomach bleeding, and ibuprofen can reduce the heart-protecting effect of low-dose aspirin. Alcohol increases the risk of stomach irritation.
