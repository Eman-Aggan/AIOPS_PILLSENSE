Ibuprofen. Uses: Ibuprofen is a non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drug (NSAID). It relieves pain, reduces inflammation, and lowers fever. It is used for headache, dental pain, period pain, joint and muscle pain, and cold or flu symptoms.
How to take: Take ibuprofen with food or milk to protect the stomach. For adults, the usual over-the-counter dose is 200 mg to 400 mg every 4 to 6 hours, and the maximum is 1200 mg in 24 hours unless a doctor says otherwise. Use the lowest dose for the shortest time needed.
Who should not take it: Do not take ibuprofen if you have a stomach ulcer or stomach bleeding, severe heart, kidney or liver failure, or if you have had asthma or an allergic reaction after taking aspirin or other NSAIDs. Avoid it in the last 3 months of pregnancy unless a doctor advises it.
Side effects: Common side effects are indigestion, stomach pain, nausea, and heartburn. Stop the medicine and seek medical help if you have black stools, vomiting blood, severe stomach pain, swelling, or breathing problems.
Interactions: Do not take ibuprofen together with aspirin or other NSAIDs because the risk of stomach bleeding increases. Ibuprofen increases the risk of bleeding when taken with warfarin and should usually be avoided. It may also reduce the effect of blood pressure medicines and can affect kidney function when combined with some of them. It can raise the level of methotrexate in the blood.
