Amoxicillin. Uses: Amoxicillin is a penicillin-type antibiotic. It treats bacterial infections such as chest infections, ear infections, sinus infections, urinary tract infections, and dental infections. It does not work against viral infections such as colds and flu.
How to take: Take amoxicillin exactly as prescribed by the doctor. It can be taken with or without food. Swallow capsules whole with water. Finish the full course even if you feel better, because stopping early may allow the bacteria to return and can contribute to antibiotic resistance.
Who should not take it: Do not take amoxicillin if you are allergic to penicillin or similar antibiotics. Tell your doctor if you have kidney problems or have ever had a rash while taking antibiotics.
Side effects: Common side effects are nausea, diarrhoea, and a mild skin rash. Stop the medicine and get emergency help if you have swelling of the face or throat, difficulty breathing, or a severe widespread rash, as these can be signs of a serious allergic reaction. Severe or bloody diarrhoea should also be reported to a doctor.
Interactions: Amoxicillin can increase the blood-thinning effect of warfarin, so blood tests may be needed more often. It can increase the level of methotrexate in the body. Taking it with allopurinol may make a skin rash more likely. Tell your doctor about all medicines you take.
