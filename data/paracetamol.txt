Paracetamol. Uses: Paracetamol is a painkiller and fever reducer. It is used for headache, toothache, period pain, muscle aches, cold and flu symptoms, and fever.
How to take: Adults usually take 500 mg to 1000 mg every 4 to 6 hours. Leave at least 4 hours between doses. Never take more than 4000 mg (4 g) in 24 hours. Children's doses depend on age and weight and must be checked on the product label.
Warnings: Taking too much paracetamol can cause serious liver damage, sometimes without early symptoms. Many cold and flu products also contain paracetamol, so check labels to avoid taking two products with paracetamol at the same time. Be careful with alcohol, and ask a doctor if you have liver or kidney problems.
Side effects: Side effects are rare. Stop and get urgent medical help if you develop a skin rash, swelling of the face or lips, or difficulty breathing, as these may be signs of an allergic reaction.
Interactions: Regular daily use of paracetamol for several days can increase the effect of warfarin and raise bleeding risk, so tell your doctor if you take warfarin. Paracetamol is generally considered a safer pain option than ibuprofen or aspirin for people taking warfarin when used occasionally at normal doses.
